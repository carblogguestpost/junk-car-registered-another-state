## Why Cars Get Stuck in Sand

- **Sand is not like asphalt**. It shifts under weight, and its grip depends on moisture, grain size, and how tightly it is packed. Vehicles usually get stuck for a few common reasons:
- **Tyre pressure too high**. Hard tyres sink instead of floating on top of the sand. 
- **Too much wheelspin**. Spinning digs the wheels deeper and can bury the axles. 
- **Stopping on a soft slope**. Starting again from a stop on loose sand is difficult.
- **Wrong vehicle for the terrain**. Low-clearance cars and many crossovers are not designed for dunes. 
- **Heavy loads**. Extra passengers and gear increase the sinking effect.

## First Steps the Moment You Feel the Car Sinking

The most important rule is to stop spinning the wheels. Pressing harder on the accelerator almost always makes things worse.

  1. **Stop and switch off the throttle**. Do not keep revving.
  2. **Get out and look**. Check how deep each wheel is and whether the underside is resting on sand.
  3. **Check the surroundings**. Note the direction of firmer ground, the slope, and the time of day.
  4. **Make sure everyone has water**. In UAE heat, water matters more than the car.

## What You Can Safely Try Yourself

If the vehicle is only slightly stuck and you have the right basics, you can try a few careful steps:

  1. **Reduce tyre pressure gradually**. Many off-road drivers lower pressure to around 15 to 20 psi in soft sand to widen the contact patch. Do not go too low, or the tyre may come off        the rim. Carry a compressor to re-inflate before returning to the road. 
  2. **Dig out the sand**. Clear sand from in front of the tyres and from under the chassis using a small shovel or your hands. 
  3. **Use recovery boards**. Place them under the driven wheels at a shallow angle.
  4. **Move gently**. Use a low gear, light throttle, and smooth steering. Reverse along your own tracks if possible. 
  5. **Rock the vehicle**. Small forward and backward movements can build momentum, but stop if the wheels begin to dig.

## What You Should Never Do

  - **Never keep flooring the throttle**. You risk burying the vehicle and overheating the engine or gearbox. 
  - **Never let friends tow you with a rope or chain that is not rated for recovery**. Snatch straps and proper shackle points exist for a reason, and a broken line can cause serious          injury.
  - **Never walk far from the vehicle**. The vehicle is easier to find than a person in the dunes. 
  - **Never ignore overheating signs**. If the gauge climbs, stop and let the engine rest.

## When to Call for Professional Desert Recovery

Call for help straight away if:
  - The car is buried to the axles or the underside is resting on sand 
  - You are on a steep slope or soft dune face
  - There is no firm ground nearby 
  - You do not have recovery gear 
  - Anyone is tired, dehydrated, or unwell The sun is setting and visibility is dropping

Professional desert recovery teams use vehicles with winches, sand ladders, and high-lift jacks designed for exactly this kind of job. They also know how to pull a vehicle out in the direction that avoids more damage.

## How to Help the Recovery Team Find You

Phone signal can be weak in the desert, so prepare early:
  - Send your live location by WhatsApp as soon as you realise you are stuck. 
  - Share the road you left, the entry point, and any landmark such as a camp, a sign, or a power line. 
  - Keep your phone in battery-saver mode and carry a power bank. 
  - Stay with the vehicle, preferably in the shade of the car. 
  - Switch on hazard lights and keep a high-visibility item in view.

If you have no signal, climb a nearby high point carefully to try again. Do not wander away from the car.

## Preparing Before You Go

Most sand recoveries are avoidable with simple preparation:

  - Go with at least two vehicles when possible. 
  - Carry a tyre pressure gauge, compressor, shovel, recovery boards, and a rated tow strap. 
  - Bring at least a few liters of water per person, plus snacks. 
  - Tell someone your route and expected return time. 
  - Check the weather and avoid driving in the hottest hours of summer. 
  - Fill the tank before you leave and keep spare fuel if your trip is long.

## Protecting Your Vehicle During Extraction

A careful recovery avoids unnecessary damage. Professionals will:
  - Attach to proper recovery points, not bumpers or plastic trim
  - Use a controlled pull to avoid sudden jerks
  - Clear sand from around the tyres and underbody first
  - Keep everyone at a safe distance from the strap during the pull

After the vehicle is out, check for leaks, damaged undertrays, and warning lights. Sand can clog air filters and radiators, so a quick inspection is wise.

**What if there is no phone signal?** Stay with the vehicle, conserve water, and try to reach higher ground for signal. Another vehicle in your group can drive to a signal area if safe.
